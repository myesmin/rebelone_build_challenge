### Subprime car loans turning into repossessions and deficiency debt
**Verdict: Watch.** The problem is real, structural and venture-shaped, but the computed score (3.22) is below the 3.5 Pursue bar and no buyer is confirmed yet.

**What's happening:** Complaints rose from 16,075 to 21,911 (1.36x vs 1.21x baseline). Two issues dominate:
- **Loan management (6,619):** billing problems and add-on products.
- **Repossession (6,005):** about 2,000 complaints about balances owed *after* the car is sold.

Another 1,682 people report they couldn't get their title after paying off the loan.

**Who's hurting:** Hourly workers with thin or damaged credit in car-dependent Sunbelt metros (FL, TX, GA rank high). The top lenders (Santander, Westlake, Credit Acceptance) are subprime specialists, so these borrowers likely carry high-APR loans bundled with GAP or warranty add-ons. Losing the car often means losing the commute, and possibly the job.

**Root cause (inferred):** High vehicle prices and rates have stretched payments beyond what borrowers can carry. Lenders are denying requests to lower payments (998 complaints), which pushes more loans to repossession. That repossession then creates a second debt and a credit-report hit.

**Today's workaround:** Borrowers miss payments, call servicers who say no, and lose the car. Some pay reinstatement fees to get it back. Others ignore the deficiency balance until it goes to collections. Few know that unused add-on premiums are often refundable.

**Venture ideas**
1. **KeepRolling:** A pre-repossession refinance marketplace.
   - It flags borrowers who are 30+ days late (self-referred or through an employer benefit).
   - It routes them to credit unions and CDFIs that refinance into lower payments.
   - Lenders pay per funded loan; employers pay per seat as a retention benefit.
2. **AddOn Refund:** Software that finds unused GAP, warranty and service-contract premiums after payoff, repossession or trade-in.
   - It files the cancellations and also tracks title release.
   - Revenue is a contingency fee on recovered refunds or a white-label fee from legal aid and credit counselors.

**Why now:** There is no grant tailwind; no matching federal funding was found. My inference is that reduced federal enforcement leaves families with fewer advocates, which favors private tools. Expanding electronic lien and title systems make title tracking and refund automation technically easier.

**Noise check:**
- **Single-company spike: pass.** The top company holds 10%, and 539 companies have complaints.
- **Short spike: pass.** The issues are structural (fees, deficiencies, add-ons), not an outage or migration.
- **Policy-only fix: partial pass.** Deficiency rules need legislators, but refinancing, refunds and title tracking don't.
- **Catch-all bucket: pass.**
- **Volume artifact: minor flag.** The 1,918 credit-report complaints may include credit-repair template filings, so discount that slice.

**Biggest risk:** Credit unions may decline to refinance borrowers who are already delinquent, leaving KeepRolling without supply.

**Next validation step:**
- Call 10 credit unions and CDFIs in FL, TX and GA to ask whether they'd pay per funded rescue refinance and at what credit floor.
- At the same time, run $500 in "behind on car payment" ads to a waitlist page and measure cost per signup.
