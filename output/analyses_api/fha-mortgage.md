### FHA homeowners falling behind and getting stuck in loss mitigation

**Verdict: Pursue.** This is a structural, multi-servicer distress signal among core LMI homeowners, and both sides of the fix (helping borrowers and helping counselors) can be productized.

**What's happening:** FHA mortgage complaints rose from 4,200 to 7,532 (1.79x), ahead of the 1.59x baseline. That lead is modest, but it sits on a high-LMI product. The dominant issue is "struggling to pay" (3,113), and the biggest sub-issue is *communicating with the servicer about modification/forbearance/foreclosure* (1,360). Escrow, taxes or insurance problems (898) lead the payment-process complaints.

**Who's hurting:** First-time buyers with low down payments or modest credit, concentrated in TX, FL and GA. Think warehouse workers, CNAs and school staff. FHA is often the only mortgage they qualify for, and the home is their main asset.

**Root cause (inferred):**
- **Escrow shocks.** Homeowners insurance premiums and property reassessments are rising, especially in FL and TX, which pushes up monthly payments.
- **Thin cushions.** Recent FHA buyers bought at high prices and have little equity to absorb increases.
- **Re-defaults.** Pandemic-era modifications may be failing again.
- **Process friction.** HUD's recent revisions to the FHA loss-mitigation waterfall have likely added confusion. Subservicers (Shellpoint, LoanCare) and servicing transfers compound it.

**Today's workaround:** Borrowers repeatedly call servicers, resend documents that get "lost," or wait weeks for an overloaded HUD-approved housing counselor. Many give up, drain savings, or take high-cost credit to cover escrow shortages.

**Venture ideas**
1. **WorkoutDesk**: case-management software for housing counselors and legal aid.
   - It assembles complete loss-mitigation packages, checks FHA waterfall eligibility, and tracks servicer response deadlines.
   - It also auto-generates escalation letters when deadlines are missed.
   - Revenue is per-seat SaaS paid by counseling agencies (fundable under HUD counseling grants) and state HFAs. A second tier sells "complete-file" submissions to servicers, who lower their cost per workout.
2. **EscrowGuard**: an early-warning and cost-cutting service for FHA homeowners.
   - It forecasts the next escrow analysis 90 days ahead and re-shops homeowners insurance.
   - It also files property-tax appeals.
   - Revenue comes from insurance agency commissions and a contingency share of tax savings, so the family pays nothing upfront. B2B versions go to HFAs and credit unions as a retention benefit.

**Why now:**
- Insurance premiums are spiking in the Sun Belt.
- FHA delinquency is elevated (inferred from complaint mix; confirm with MBA data).
- HUD loss-mitigation rules are changing.
- HUD's FY2026 Comprehensive Housing Counseling NOFO (**HSG-2600-DC-0033**) is a relevant buyer signal. It funds the agencies that would license WorkoutDesk, though it doesn't pay startups directly.

**Noise check:**
- Single-company spike: **pass.** The top company has 10%, spread across 331 companies.
- Short spike: **pass.** The issues are structural (affordability, escrow, loss mitigation), not an outage. Servicing transfers add some operational noise.
- Policy-only fix: **pass.** Navigation, documentation and cost reduction are solvable without regulators.
- Catch-all bucket: **pass.** The customer is clearly identifiable.
- Volume artifact: **pass.** Credit-reporting issues are only ~6% of volume.

**Biggest risk:** Counseling agencies have thin, grant-dependent budgets and servicers buy slowly, so revenue could lag the need.

**Next validation step:** Call 15 HUD-approved counseling agencies in TX, FL and GA. Ask about caseload growth, hours spent per loss-mitigation file, and willingness to pay $X per seat. In parallel, run a $500 Facebook landing-page test for EscrowGuard aimed at FL and TX homeowners and measure the signup rate.
