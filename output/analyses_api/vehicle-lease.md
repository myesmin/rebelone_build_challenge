### Car lease billing, lease-end and repossession problems

**Verdict: Watch.** The problems are real, structural and widespread, but the computed score is 3.17 (below 3.5), there is no grant-backed buyer, and lease-heavy households skew less LMI than the product's LMI score suggests.

**What's happening:** Lease complaints rose from 2,310 to 3,479 (1.51x, versus a 1.34x baseline). Three issues of similar size account for about 73% of complaints: billing and fees (892), lease-end problems (875) and repossession (779). The end-of-lease complaints are mostly wear/mileage charges, title delays and early-termination fees. There were also 153 "loan opened without consent" complaints, which suggests dealer-side fraud or paperwork abuse.

**Who's hurting:** Inferred, since no narratives are available. These are working families who leased because the monthly payment was the only one that fit. They include buyers with thin or subprime credit (Santander is #2) and households in CA, FL, TX and GA where driving is non-optional. When they lose the car, they often lose the commute and then the job.

**Root cause (inferred):** Leases written in 2022–23 at inflated prices are now maturing. Exit costs and buyout prices feel punitive at the same time that tariffs and high rates make replacing the car expensive. Servicers (mostly captives such as Hyundai, Toyota and Honda) handle lease-end disputes and delinquency with little room for negotiation.

**Today's workaround:** Lessees pay disputed wear charges, extend the lease at bad terms, or fall behind and get repossessed. A repossession can leave a deficiency balance (240 complaints) and a damaged credit report (464 reporting/dispute complaints). Most people never learn about third-party buyout financing.

**Venture ideas**
1. **LeaseExit**: An app for lessees about 90 days from turn-in.
   - It provides a guided self-inspection with timestamped photos, a fee-dispute letter generator, and a buyout-vs-return-vs-sell calculator.
   - It routes buyouts to credit unions and CDFIs, which pay $200–400 per funded loan.
   - It is free to consumers.
2. **KeepRolling**: An employer benefit for hourly-heavy employers.
   - Workers get a billing-error audit, help negotiating reinstatement before repossession, and a small bridge loan repaid via payroll.
   - Employers pay $3–5 per employee per month, justified by lower turnover from lost transportation.

**Why now:** A wave of leases is maturing, and car prices are rising under tariffs. Credit unions are hungry for auto loans. There are no relevant federal grants. Any buyer is commercial (lenders, employers), not public.

**Noise check:**
- Single-company spike: **pass**. The top company holds 11%, spread across 211 companies.
- Short spike: **pass**. The issues are structural (fees, titles, repossession), not an outage. Still worth checking whether Hyundai's volume reflects a servicing change.
- Policy-only fix: **pass**. Deficiency rules need regulators, but disputes, buyout refinancing and pre-repossession help are serviceable.
- Catch-all bucket: **pass**.
- Volume artifact: **caution**. About 13% of complaints are credit-report disputes and could include template filings.

**Biggest risk:** Lessees may be less LMI and less reachable than borrowers on auto loans. Lender referral economics may only work for buyouts, not for distressed accounts.

**Next validation step:** Run $500 of targeted "lease ending soon?" ads in CA, FL and TX with a waitlist for a free inspection kit. In parallel, call 10 credit unions to get committed per-loan referral fees for lease buyouts.
