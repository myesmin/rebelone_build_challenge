### Buy-now-pay-later debt stacking among working families

**Verdict: Watch.** A real, structural BNPL repayment problem that software could address, but there is no grant-backed buyer and growth is only modestly ahead of baseline.

**What's happening:** Installment-loan complaints rose from 6,353 to 11,541 (1.82x), compared with 1.61x for all non-credit-reporting complaints. The top issues are about repayment, not origination: problems making payments (2,787), unexpected fees or interest (2,562), add-on products (1,206) and payoff trouble (853). Affirm, Klarna and Block (Afterpay) are three of the top five companies, so this is mostly a BNPL story, not a payday story.

**Who's hurting:** *Inferred:* hourly and gig workers in CA, TX, FL and GA who use pay-in-4 plans for groceries, car repairs and school costs. They carry several plans across providers, each autodrafting a debit card on its own schedule. When a draft lands before payday, they get a late fee from the lender and possibly an overdraft fee from the bank.

**Root cause (inferred):** BNPL has spread from discretionary retail into everyday spending, and families now "stack" plans that no single provider can see. Federal oversight eased after the CFPB withdrew its 2024 BNPL interpretive rule in 2025. Meanwhile, providers have begun reporting BNPL activity to credit bureaus, so missed payments now hurt credit scores.

**Today's workaround:** Spreadsheets, calendar reminders, skipping bills, or covering shortfalls with payday loans and overdrafts. Each of these adds cost or risk.

**Venture ideas**
1. **StackSync**: An app that pulls in every BNPL plan (through email parsing and open banking), maps due dates against the user's paycheck, and warns about collisions. It can also auto-request reschedules where providers allow it. It would be sold to employers and credit unions as a financial-wellness benefit, priced per member per month, and free to the worker.
2. **Bundle Loan**: Lending-as-a-service for credit unions and CDFIs. It consolidates a member's BNPL balances into one small-dollar installment loan (similar to a PAL) that is reported to bureaus, so on-time payments build credit. Revenue would come from platform fees plus a share of interest.

**Why now:** BNPL reporting to bureaus and new credit-score models that include BNPL raise the stakes of a missed payment. With federal BNPL rules pulled back, a private-market fix is the likely path. **No matching federal grants were found (buyer score 0.0)**, so any buyer case rests on employers and credit unions, which is unproven.

**Noise check:**
- **Single-company spike:** Pass. The top company (Affirm) holds 17%; the top five hold 34%.
- **Short spike:** Mostly pass. The issues are structural (fees, payment timing). Paramount GR Holdings (771) needs a check for a firm-specific event.
- **Policy-only fix:** Pass. Software and better credit products can help without new rules.
- **Catch-all bucket:** Pass. There is an identifiable customer.
- **Volume artifact:** Uncertain. Some growth may come from BNPL complaints newly being routed into this sub-product.

**Biggest risk:** BNPL providers block data access or build their own cross-provider tools, leaving no defensible position for a third party.

**Next validation step:** Run $500 of targeted ads in TX, FL and GA to a StackSync waitlist that asks how many active plans the person has, and aim for at least 3% signup. In parallel, cold-call 15 credit unions about Bundle Loan to gauge demand. Also pull the Paramount complaints to rule out a one-off event.
