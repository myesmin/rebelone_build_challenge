### Payday & cash-advance borrowers getting hit with surprise fees and withdrawals they can't stop
**Verdict: Watch.** The pain is real, structural and widely spread, but growth barely beats the baseline, there is no grant buyer, and part of the problem needs enforcement.

**What's happening:** Complaints rose from 1,991 to 2,782 (1.4x), only modestly faster than the 1.24x baseline. Unexpected fees or interest lead the issues (904, about 32%), followed by struggling to pay (500), withdrawals borrowers can't stop (337) and wrong-day or wrong-amount debits (155). Together, payment-control problems make up about 18% of complaints.

**Who's hurting:** Hourly and gig workers in CA, TX, FL, OH and GA who have thin checking-account buffers. They use $100–$500 advances to bridge to payday. When a debit lands on the wrong day, it can trigger overdraft fees and a spiral of rollovers.

**Root cause (inferred):** The company list shows two growth channels:
- **App-based cash advances.** Dave is the #1 company, which suggests "tips," express fees and subscriptions read as hidden costs.
- **Online lenders that appear tribal-affiliated** (Rosebud, WLCC and Uprova, inferred from their names). These lenders often sit outside state rate caps.

"Received a loan you didn't apply for" (204) points to identity fraud or lead-generation abuse.

**Today's workaround:** Borrowers call their bank to stop payment, which often fails or costs about $30. Others close their accounts, take another advance to cover the first, or file complaints after the money is already gone.

**Venture ideas**
1. **DebitGuard**: A tool embedded in credit unions and neobanks that lets members revoke an ACH authorization in one tap. It generates the revocation letter, files the Reg E stop-payment and flags repeat lender attempts. Banks pay per active account because it cuts overdraft write-offs, disputes and member churn.
2. **Bridgeback**: A refinance referral network that finds members carrying high-APR advance or payday debt and routes them to credit-union small-dollar loans (NCUA PAL II, 28% APR cap) or CDFI loans. It also reports on-time payments to the credit bureaus. Credit unions pay $75–150 per funded loan as an acquisition cost, and employers can sponsor it as a benefit.

**Why now:** The CFPB payday rule's payment provisions, which limit lenders after two consecutive failed withdrawals, took compliance effect in 2025, but federal enforcement is weak, so a private tool can fill the gap. Open-banking data makes it easier to detect payday and advance debits. No federal grants matched. The weak buyer signal is a real gap.

**Noise check:**
- Single-company spike: **pass** (top company is 6% across 330 companies).
- Short spike: **pass** (the issues are structural fees and payment control, not an outage).
- Policy-only fix: **partial fail** (pricing at tribal lenders needs enforcement, though payment control and refinancing are fixable with products).
- Catch-all: **pass**.
- Volume artifact: **uncertain** (Dave-type advances may be newly filed under "payday," and "didn't apply" could include template or fraud filings).

**Biggest risk:** Banks and credit unions may see stop-payment tooling as a cost center and refuse to pay for it.
