### Disputed move-out charges sent to collections
**Verdict: Watch.** The signal is real and structural, but the computed score (3.21) is below the 3.5 Pursue bar, growth is modest, and template-filing contamination hasn't been ruled out.

**What's happening:** Rental-debt collection complaints rose from 10,630 to 12,181 (1.15x, vs 1.02x baseline). The core problem is disputed balances, not harassment:
- "Debt is not yours": 3,492
- "Wrong amount": 2,855
- "Debt was paid": 993
- "Not enough information to verify": 1,448

**Who's hurting:** Renters who recently left large multifamily complexes. This is inferred from the top collectors, which are specialty rental-debt firms like National Credit Systems and Columbia Debt Recovery that typically serve big property managers. The TX/FL/GA concentration points to Sun Belt hourly workers who move often. A collections tradeline tied to a past lease can block their next apartment approval (703 complaints cite credit threats).

**Root cause (inferred):** Large landlords appear to bill post-move-out charges (cleaning, damage, early termination, fees) months later and sell or place them with collectors without itemized ledgers. Tenant screening then turns these disputed balances into housing barriers.

**Today's workaround:** Tenants write dispute letters with no move-out evidence, pay unverified balances to get approved elsewhere, or wait for overloaded legal aid. All three tend to lose money or time.

**Venture ideas**
1. **MoveOut Proof**: A renter app for timestamped move-in and move-out walkthroughs that auto-generates Reg F validation requests and credit-bureau disputes.
   - **Distribution:** Free to renters; licensed to legal aid groups and tenant organizations.
   - **Revenue:** Per-seat fees funded by eviction and housing grants, plus a white-label version for renters-insurance and deposit-alternative providers.
2. **LedgerClear**: Pre-collection reconciliation software for property managers. It sends tenants itemized charges with photos and offers payment plans before an account goes to a collector.
   - **Who pays:** Landlords, via a SaaS fee or a recovery share below the collector's contingency cut.
   - **Pitch to landlords:** Higher recovery, fewer CFPB and AG complaints.

**Why now:**
- CFPB Reg F validation-notice requirements map directly to the 1,448 verification complaints.
- Rental junk fees and tenant screening are under scrutiny.
- **Grants:** HUD PDR-2600-DC-0079 (Eviction Protection, forecast) is a moderately relevant buyer path for tenant legal services, though it is aimed at eviction, not post-tenancy debt. O-OVC-2026-172790 (human trafficking) is a keyword false match and should be ignored.

**Noise check:**
- **Single-company spike:** Pass. The top company holds 23%, though the top five hold 58% and 358 firms appear.
- **Short spike:** Pass. Issues are structural (wrong amount, not owed), not operational.
- **Policy-only fix:** Pass, partially. Documentation and dispute tooling help without new rules.
- **Catch-all bucket:** Pass.
- **Volume artifact:** Uncertain. "Debt is not yours" is a common credit-repair template sub-issue and needs checking.

**Biggest risk:** Landlords and collectors profit from opaque balances, and struggling renters have little ability to pay, so the business depends on grant-funded or insurer intermediaries.

**Next validation step:**
1. Download public CFPB narratives for the top 5 collectors and code 200 of them: genuine move-out disputes vs template text. This also resolves the volume-artifact question.
2. In parallel, interview 10 TX/GA legal aid or tenant organizations about willingness to pay for a dispute tool.
